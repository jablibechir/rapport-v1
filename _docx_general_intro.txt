Problemstatement
Recruitment has become a critical and complex function for modern organizations, particularly in environments characterized by high competition and rapid growth. Human resources departments are required to attract qualified candidates, manage large volumes of applications, and make accurate hiring decisions within limited timeframes. However, traditional recruitment methods continue to present several major challenges, including:
The increasing number of applications received for each job opening
The manual and time-consuming processing of CVs and candidate information
The difficulty of efficiently matching candidates’ skills with job requirements
The lack of centralized platforms to manage job offers, applications, and recruiter interactions
According to studies on electronic recruitment tools, manual recruitment processes are often repetitive, error-prone, and inefficient, especially when recruiters face large-scale CV screening and multiple recruitment stages. These constraints make it difficult for HR professionals to maintain responsiveness, objectivity, and consistency in hiring decisions. [A Conceptu...ment Tools | PDF]
In the context of Sopra HR Software, which operates in a sector where technological innovation and service quality are essential, these challenges highlight the need for a more structured, automated, and intelligent recruitment approach. The absence of a unified and intelligent recruitment portal can lead to fragmented information, longer hiring cycles, and reduced efficiency for recruiters.

Existing System Analysis
The current recruitment practices in many organizations, including enterprise environments like Sopra HR Software, often rely on multiple disconnected tools and manual operations. Job offers may be published across different platforms, while applications are received through emails, job boards, or internal systems. Recruiters must manually review CVs, shortlist candidates, coordinate interviews, and track feedback.
As highlighted in studies on online recruitment systems, handling large volumes of applications without an automated applicant tracking system makes it difficult to identify suitable candidates efficiently and consistently. This fragmentation slows down the recruitment process and increases the workload on HR teams.
2-2 Critique of the Existing System and Proposed Solution
2-2-1 Critique of the Existing System
Several limitations characterize the existing recruitment approach:
Lack of centralization: Candidate data and job offers are spread across multiple platforms.
Time-consuming CV screening: Manual sorting of resumes increases processing time and recruiter workload.
Reduced decision accuracy: Comparing candidates becomes difficult without structured evaluation tools.
Limited process visibility: Recruiters struggle to track application status and recruitment stages effectively.
These limitations reduce the overall efficiency of recruitment activities and prevent recruiters from focusing on strategic HR tasks.

Tools to assist recruiters in evaluating candidates and making decisions
A unified platform facilitating interaction between recruiters and applicants
By adopting an intelligent recruitment portal, Sopra HR Software can streamline hiring workflows, reduce recruitment time, and enhance the effectiveness and reliability of the recruitment process. 3.2 Scrum methodology
Scrum is widely recognized as a project management framework that facilitates the efficient development of complex systems. It relies on an iterative and incremental approach, organized into short, time-boxed cycles known as sprints, typically lasting from a few days up to one month. Each sprint is guided by a clearly defined objective and results in the delivery of a potentially usable product increment.
Agile methodologies, particularly Scrum, are built upon four fundamental values:
Individuals and interactions.
A functional and operational product.
Customer collaboration.
Responsiveness to change.
This framework allows teams to adapt effectively to evolving requirements while promoting continuous improvement and maintaining high quality throughout the development process.
SCRUM actors
Scrum is built around three main roles, each with clearly defined responsibilities:
Product Owner: The Product Owner is responsible for defining the product vision and representing stakeholder needs. This role involves identifying project requirements, translating them into tasks, and prioritizing the product backlog to maximize value delivery.
Scrum Master: The Scrum Master ensures that the Scrum framework is properly understood and applied by all participants. This role facilitates communication between the Product Owner and the development team, removes obstacles, and ensures that the project progresses efficiently according to Agile principles.
Development Team: The development team is composed of 3 to 9 members who collaborate daily to design, develop, and deliver the expected system features at the end of each sprint.

3.3 Product Backlog
The Product Backlog is a structured list that contains all the features, functionalities, and requirements expected from the product. Scrum does not impose a specific format or naming convention for backlog items; however, they are mostly expressed as user stories or use cases. In a Product Backlog, these items are organized according totheir potential implementation order, making prioritization a key aspect of iterative development. The Product Backlog serves as a guide for the development team and remains flexible, evolving throughout the project as requirements change. Its creation and ongoing refinement are collaborative activities involving both the Product Owner and the development team. This artifact brings together a prioritized set of functional elements, most often defined as user stories. Each user story is characterized by several key attributes that facilitate planning and implementation:
Priority: The level of importance assigned by the Product Owner reflects the business value and urgency of the feature.
Initial Estimation: A preliminary evaluation of the effort required to implement the user story in relation to others, generally expressed in terms of time, such as days.
Sprint Backlog
Commonly referred to as the Sprint Backlog, this artifact represents the list of work that the team commits to completing during a sprint. It is often broken down into a set of more detailed and operational tasks derived from the selected user stories. The sprint Backlog was initially created during the Sprint Planning Meeting, but it remains flexible and is removed by the development team as requirements evolve or new constraints arise. Each task within the Sprint Backlog is monitored during the sprint and reflects the remaining effort needed to achieve the sprint objectives.
